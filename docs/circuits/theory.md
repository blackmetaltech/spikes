# Theory wing: dynamics, rates, memory, tuning

Levels in `engine/src/tasks/` on the Bestiary scaffolding (`bestiary_a.h`, plus `theory.h` for the levels that read a cell's own parameters). Reference circuits: `circuits/*.json`, generated by `circuits/tools/gen_theory.py`. Cheap circuits that must fail: `engine/tests/cheap_rows_f.inc`; contract and reference tests: `engine/tests/tasks_theory_test.cpp`. Design and what is simplified: `docs/DESIGN-THEORY.md`.

## Dynamics (`dynamics`)

| Level | Teaches | Mechanism the check demands | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| `d_class` | class I and class II excitability | a two-variable cell whose resting state, read from its phase plane, is lost as a resonant focus (Hopf), not a node (saddle-node) | 1 Izhikevich cell (a 0.1, b 0.26, c -65, d 2) |
| `d_phasic` | a stable fixed point that survives the current: phasic firing | a stable fixed point at the held current; behaviour: spikes at the step, none later | 1 Izhikevich cell (a 0.02, b 0.25, c -65, d 6), step 0.5 |
| `d_switch` | bistability: one cell, two stable states | a stable fixed point at the holding current, all three sensors reaching the cell; behaviour: on after `set`, off after `reset`, twice | 1 Izhikevich cell (a 0.02, b 0.2, c -50, d 0), hold 2.5 |

* The staircase of `d_class` is scaled to the cell: 70 % to 130 % of the current at which its own resting state is lost (`theory::onset`), so the sensor weight cannot turn a slowly-starting cell into an abrupt one (tested at weights x0.1 and x30).
* The resonance threshold of `d_class` (the damped oscillation of the resting state must be at least 15 Hz) is a game value that separates the Izhikevich resonator and fast-spiking presets from the regular-spiking default, whose rest is a nearly degenerate spiral (a Bogdanov-Takens neighbourhood).
* `d_switch`: the `reset` sensor carries a negative current (the level is scaled by -1 in the task), so its weight stays positive in the lab; the window of holding currents that works for the reference is about 1.5 to 3.5 (the cell fires on its own from 4).
* Izhikevich cells start at their resting fixed point (`rest_state`), not at NEST's U = 0.

## Rate models (`rates`)

| Level | Teaches | Mechanism the check demands | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| `r_oscillate` | the Wilson-Cowan pair | two rate units; drive reaches E; E > E, E > I positive, I > E negative; behaviour: a rhythm after the drive, I following E | 2 `threshold_lin_rate_ipn` (E tau 10, I tau 20, ceiling 1): E > E +5, E > I +0.8, I > E -6, drive 0.2 |
| `r_choose` | competition between populations | two rate units, each excites itself, each inhibits the other; behaviour: a small advantage is amplified, held when the inputs are equal, overturned by a strong challenger | 2 units: self +1.25, mutual -1, inputs 0.5/0.45, then equal, then 0.2/0.9 |
| `r_meanfield` | mean field and where it holds | spiking E-I loop with several sparse random inputs per cell, driven by `ext`; siegert E-I loop driven by a driving siegert unit; behaviour: the two rates agree at three drive levels | 200 + 50 `iaf_psc_delta` cells (K 60 and 20, J 0.3 and -1.5 mV) and 3 siegert units with the same J and K |

* Rate units are NEST `threshold_lin_rate_ipn` with a ceiling (`alpha` 1) and no noise (`sigma` 0): `tau dr/dt = -r + mu + min(max(g (sum_j w_j r_j - theta), 0), alpha)`. The drive writes `mu`, so it passes the unit's nonlinearity unrectified; this is why the winner-take-all window of `r_choose` is narrow and the level asks for a held choice, not a flip-flop at equal strength.
* `r_meanfield` compares the mean rate of the spiking E cells (`net`, Hz per cell) with the rate of the siegert unit it is wired to predict (`mf`). The reference recurrence is strong enough that the prediction without the inhibition is off by a factor of two or more, so a prediction that ignores the loop fails; the rows of `cheap_rows_f.inc` pin that for K and J mismatches.
* The agreement band is 15 % of the larger rate plus 1.5 Hz (guided 25 %, researcher 10 %). With 200 cells the spiking rates carry a sampling error of about 2 %.

## Attractor memory (`memory`)

| Level | Teaches | Mechanism the check demands | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| `h_complete` | a partial cue recalls the pattern | several cells with recurrent excitation, a cue that reaches some but not all, roughly symmetric weights; behaviour: silent before the cue, then the whole pattern (including the uncued cells) fires and stays lit | groups C (3, cued) and R (5), all to all +400 |
| `h_two` | two patterns, no blends | disjoint patterns, each with recurrent excitation, inhibition both ways, symmetric weights; behaviour: cue A lights only A, cue B overwrites it, both cues at once end in exactly one | groups A and B (4 cells each): +375 inside, -500 between |
| `h_learn` | storage by plasticity | recurrent excitation through `stdp_synapse` among the pattern's cells, symmetric at the start, `show` reaching every cell, `cue` only some; behaviour: no completion before the exposure, weights grown at least 1.5 x, completion after | C and R as above, stdp weight 80, lambda 0.0015, alpha 0.5, Wmax 800 |

* A pattern is a group (or two): the weight matrix is block-structured, not an outer-product rule over +1 / -1 vectors. The lab connects groups all to all (or with random sparse wiring).
* "Symmetric" is checked between groups: every projection has a partner back of about the same mean weight (within a factor of two). `h_learn` checks it at the start; plasticity is free to break it later.
* Persistent activity is at 100 Hz or more (no balanced inhibition, no adaptation): it is an attractor, not a model of delay activity.
* `h_learn`: learning is compressed (a 1.2 s exposure at 60 Hz, lambda 0.0015); with lambda 0.0025 the 200 ms cue itself writes the pattern and `naive` fails (a cheap row pins this at lambda 0.03).

## Tuning and decoding (`tuning`)

| Level | Teaches | Mechanism the check demands | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| `t_tuned` | a tuned cell from convergence | the cell `resp` reads is not an afferent, hears some afferents by excitation and not all; behaviour: a clear response at 90 degrees, at least 3 x the response at 0 and 1.4 x at 45 and 135 | afferents in groups A1 (4), A2 (1), A3 (3), one cell listening to A2 |
| `t_sharpen` | sharper tuning from inhibition | cells driven by the afferents, not the afferents, with a net inhibitory loop among them; behaviour: the response of cells 45 degrees from the shown orientation is at most 48 % of the peak | 8 afferents, 8 cells one to one, all-to-all inhibition -2500 and self-excitation +2500 |
| `t_decode` | the population vector | `heading` (ring readout) reads at least 8 cells that are not afferents and listen to them; behaviour: mean decoding error at most 25 degrees over six random directions | 12 afferents, 12 cells one to one |

* The afferents are `tuned_poisson` inputs: von Mises tuning, Poisson spikes, one generator per afferent; the preferred angle of afferent k of N is the period times k / N, in circuit order. The levels use a `raw` sensor (the angle is not scaled by the episode's small gain noise).
* `t_sharpen` reads the response of every cell relative to the orientation shown (the cell preferring it, and the two cells 45 degrees away) over twelve trials, because one cell's spike count is too noisy. A uniform inhibition without self-excitation sharpens less than the threshold in most seeds: the level asks for competition.
* The random directions of `t_decode` come from the episode seed (a different draw per seed, deterministic).
* The Analysis screen has a seventh tab, "STA": a seeded Gaussian white-noise current is written into the first cell of the chosen population every ms for up to 20 s and the stimulus that preceded each spike is averaged (`theory::spike_triggered_average`, tested in `test_theory`). The mean and the standard deviation of the noise are a per-model table in `analysis.gd`, near the cell's threshold.
